# Blocky story (`blocky-story`)

Blocky figures in bright, code-built places: a cast of drawn-faced characters, eight locations, hard cuts, and a camera that
pushes, pulls, orbits or cranes. Every shot carries a gesture, and the caption band is drawn from the voice's own word
times. Engine `html-capture`, 1080x1920, short only.

The Roblox look has three kits (three.js, Remotion, hybrid). This is **one style**: the engine is an implementation detail,
and the kit that already renders one frame per request is the one that ships.

## What the look locks, and what the request decides

Locked in `render/`:

- **The kit's own look** — vendored unchanged: `assets/engine/{core,chars,props,acting,sets,studio}.js`, its blocky figures,
  its fourteen drawn face atlases, its sky, its lighting and its caption drawing.
- **Eight locations**: plaza, schoolyard, classroom, gym, auditorium, townhall, rumah, kelas tua.
- **The camera vocabulary**: `push`, `pull`, `orbit`, `crane`, with the framing derived from the subject.
- **The cast** (`render/cast.js`): two characters per shot from the kit's presets, one gesture each, hard cuts.

Open to the request:

- how many shots and their seconds,
- the motion token per shot (`zoom-in`, `zoom-out`, `pan-left`, `orbit`, `crane`, `static`),
- the captions: their text and their windows. The words become the voice's word timings, the studio animates on them, and it
  draws the caption band from the same numbers — so picture and caption cannot drift apart.

## How it works

The kit expects a "video folder": `script.txt`, `audio/words.json`, `meta.json` and `shots.js`. A pack has none of that, so
`render/adapter.js`:

1. turns the request's captions into words with times, and answers those three `fetch` paths from memory;
2. hands the choreography to the studio through a **hook** — the single line changed in `studio.load()`, which otherwise
   imports `/video/shots.js`;
3. feeds the kit's face atlases to its texture loader from data URLs;
4. exposes `window.VENSTUDIO = { init(props), renderFrame(f) }` and shows the studio's composited canvas, which is what the
   worker photographs.

`render/page.js` is that adapter plus the kit and three.js, bundled once into one classic script (a pack page is a bare
`<script src>`, and the repo's stand-in DOM test runs page scripts that way):

```bash
node tools/bundle-blocky-page.mjs          # rebuild page.js
node tools/bundle-blocky-page.mjs --check  # fail if page.js is older than a source
```

## No binaries in the pack

The face atlases are 14 PNGs (2.2 MB). They travel as data URLs in six classic scripts (`render/assets/faces-*.js`),
generated by `tools/embed-blocky-assets.mjs` and loaded before `page.js`. Lossless on purpose: a downscaled face is a
different face. The bundle stays at ~730 KB, under the repo's 1 MB per-file rule.

## Fixture and CI

`fixtures/payload.json` is a three-shot, six-second request with Indonesian captions; `fixtures/expect.json` says what a
correct render measures (1080x1920, 24 fps, 6 s ± 0.15). The `html-capture` job in CI renders it with the vendored worker in
Chrome Headless Shell and checks size, fps, duration and frame count; `tools/html-pages.test.mjs` runs the same page in a
stand-in DOM, where it answers the job's shape and draws nothing (no WebGL there).

- The cast is eight presets; a script longer than eight shots reuses faces.
- The kit's own QA (`studio.qa()`) reports framing and read problems; it is not wired into the pack's QC yet.
